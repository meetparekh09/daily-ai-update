## Trending Papers

**MuonGS: Pretraining LLMs with Relaxed Weight-Norm Control** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37647
Introduces a Gram–Schmidt projection step into the Muon optimizer so weight norms grow at most O(√t) rather than being pinned, improving perplexity on extended training horizons.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* If you're choosing between Adam-family and Muon-family optimizers for a pretraining run, this paper gives a cleaner theoretical account of why vanilla Muon can plateau—and a practical fix that doesn't require re-tuning your whole training stack.

**SAPO: Single-Rollout Autoregressive Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19842
A PPO-style framework that unifies policy, state-value, and action-value estimation into a single causal language model for long-horizon agentic RL.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Multi-rollout RL for agents is expensive; SAPO's single-rollout design cuts compute while addressing the credit-assignment problem across long interaction sequences—relevant for anyone fine-tuning LLMs to act in tool-use environments.

**TrustAttention: Separating Instructions from Data as an Attention Problem** — https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/168253
Learns which attention heads drive instruction-following and then gates their attention away from untrusted tokens, reducing attack success from 66% (SecAlign baseline) to 9% under adaptive attacks.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Prompt injection is the top security concern for production agents that consume external content; TrustAttention offers a head-level mitigation that doesn't require retraining the base model from scratch.

**RSI Arena: Benchmarking Recursive Self-Improvement with Human Feedback** — https://huggingface.co/papers
A benchmark that lets models iteratively rewrite their own weights or prompts with human raters providing signal, measuring how far the loop can go before alignment drift.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Self-improvement loops are entering production (e.g., models writing their own eval suites); this benchmark is an early attempt to measure the risks and limits of that pattern in a controlled setting.

**Rollback: Offline World Models for Policy Improvement** — https://huggingface.co/papers
Proposes using a world model to simulate rollouts from recorded trajectories so a policy can improve without additional environment interaction.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Collecting new agent trajectories is expensive and risky in production; Rollback's offline improvement pattern is directly applicable to anyone who has a replay buffer of agent runs they haven't fully exploited yet.
