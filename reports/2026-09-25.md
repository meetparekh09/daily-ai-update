## Trending Papers

**Agent-Editing World Model: Rethinking World Modeling for LLM Agents** — [arxiv.org/abs/2609.28416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28416)
Proposes AEWM, which models how reasoning and actions shape future task progress rather than simulating raw tool responses, combining an Action Judge (classifying decisions as critical/exploratory/noisy) with State Revision (editing contaminated history).
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Task-state contamination — where stale plans and unsupported assumptions persist and corrupt later decisions — is a real production failure mode; AEWM gives a concrete architectural handle on fixing it.

**What is Missing from AI Post-Training AI: An Empirical Analysis** — [arxiv.org/abs/2608.19072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19072)
Analyzes public post-training trajectories and finds that LLM agents doing end-to-end model training lock in their strategy at the very beginning and only make local adjustments; an experience-driven scaffold fixes execution gaps (+12.6 pts GSM8K, +40.8 pts HumanEval).
*Why it matters for practitioners:* If you're building AI-assisted training pipelines, the bottleneck isn't reasoning power — it's the inability to revise high-level strategy as evidence accumulates; this paper makes that distinction concrete with a path forward.

**Agents in the Wild: Where Research Meets Deployment** — [arxiv.org/abs/2607.19336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19336)
Tutorial/survey (KDD 2026) covering multi-agent coordination, reasoning, evaluation, and applied case studies in pharmaceutical discovery and financial systems, with design patterns for production-grade agentic systems.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Rare synthesis of what actually works at scale across two real verticals, including practical mitigation strategies for common failure modes — verification pipelines, fallback mechanisms, human-in-the-loop supervision.

**AI-Research Agents in the Wild: From GitHub and arXiv to Regularities and Gaps** — [arxiv.org/abs/2609.11975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11975)
Empirical study of AI research agents deployed on GitHub and arXiv, mapping what they do well, where they get stuck, and the systematic gaps between agent behavior and researcher expectations.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Ground-truth behavioral analysis of real deployed research agents — the regularities identified here translate directly into design constraints for anyone building AI-assisted research or coding workflows.

**Demystifying Reinforcement Learning Post-Training of Language Models** — [arxiv.org/abs/2608.24949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24949)
Mechanistic breakdown of how RL post-training actually works in practice, clarifying the relationship between reward modeling, policy optimization, and the emergent capabilities seen after RLHF and RLAIF.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Cuts through the "post-training is magic" framing with concrete mechanisms — essential reading if you are designing reward signals or fine-tuning pipelines for a production model.
