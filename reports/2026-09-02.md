## Trending Papers

**Right Answer, Wrong Method: Shortcut Hacking Misleads the Evaluation of LLM Reasoning on Frontier Science Benchmarks** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02442
Discovers that 8.2–44.1% of credited "correct" answers from frontier models on hard science benchmarks are produced by invalid shortcuts (numerical enumeration, answer-first guessing), with shortcut rates reaching 37.4% at HLE difficulty.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Headline accuracy numbers on the benchmarks used to justify model selection are materially inflated; the paper also proposes expert-inspired anti-hacking judges and test-time countermeasures that practitioners can apply now.

**The Capability Frontier: Benchmarks Miss 82% of Model Performance** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26836
Studying 21 LLMs across 16 benchmarks, defines the Pareto frontier over model × generation budget and finds that single-run evaluation understates true capability by 82%, while SOTA accuracy can be matched at just 15% of standard evaluation cost.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* This challenges how model selection is done in practice; cheap ensemble strategies can match expensive single-model runs, changing the economics of both evaluation and deployment.

**Agora: Enhancing LLM Agent Reasoning Via Auction-Based Task Allocation** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09600
Introduces an incentive-compatible auction mechanism for multi-agent LLM systems where expert models and tools bid on each reasoning step based on "rectified competence," routing to the most capable and cost-efficient solver.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* The auction framing gives a concrete, principled mechanism for balancing cost vs. quality in heterogeneous multi-agent pipelines — directly applicable to production agent systems with mixed model tiers.

**Decentralized Multi-Agent Systems with Shared Context (DeLM)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10662
Parallel agents claim subtasks from a shared queue, read a verified shared context of accumulated progress, reason locally, and write back compact updates — no central orchestrator required, with gains on both software engineering and long-context tasks.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Centralized orchestration is the default pattern but creates a bottleneck; DeLM shows a concrete alternative where the shared context primitive handles coordination, and it scales better.

**Agents-K1: Towards Agent-native Knowledge Orchestration** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13669
Converts 2.46 million scientific papers into agent-native knowledge graphs with typed entity-relation-evidence structure, using a GRPO-trained 4B extraction backbone and a CLI unifying web search, multimodal graph retrieval, and cross-document traversal.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Demonstrates a concrete GRPO training recipe for building the information-extraction backbone that powers domain-specific agent RAG at scale — the training approach is the transferable insight, not just the scientific domain.
