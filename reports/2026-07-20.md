## Trending Papers

**CurateEvo: Data-Curation Evolving for Agentic Post-Training** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06140
Proposes a failure-driven framework that represents data curation strategy as executable code and iteratively rewrites it using failed trajectories from a held-out dev set, simultaneously augmenting/filtering/refining data and pruning redundant examples under a cost-aware objective.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Instead of treating data curation as a fixed preprocessing step, CurateEvo makes it adaptive — the curation policy improves every epoch based on what the model actually gets wrong, yielding +3.2 and +2.7 average score gains on ACEBench-Agent and BFCL-V4 while reducing curation overhead; this is a practical recipe for making agentic post-training cheaper and more data-efficient.

**Evidence-Grounded Verified Agentic Reasoning (EG-VAR)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12650
Introduces a Lean 4-based tool-calling architecture where a formal proof kernel is the sole authority for stamping agent-produced claims as "Verified," making hallucination structurally impossible for the certified reasoning chain.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* For agents doing scientific inference or operating in high-stakes domains, this provides a principled answer to the "how do you trust the output?" question: not post-hoc checking but kernel-level attestation, with every claim traceable to a tool-attested proof object.

**OpenThoughts-Agent: Data Recipes for Agentic Models** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24855
Provides concrete, evaluated prescriptions for composing post-training data for agentic models — what mix of tool-use traces, reasoning chains, and multi-turn trajectories produces agents that generalize vs. memorize.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* The data recipe matters as much as the architecture; this paper makes the composition choices explicit and gives empirically validated ratios rather than folklore, directly informing how teams should assemble agentic fine-tuning datasets.

**Always-On Agents: A Survey of Persistent Memory, State, and Governance in LLM Agents** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30306
Defines always-on agents as persistent-state systems that accumulate memories, task ledgers, permissions, credentials, commitments, and audit records across interactions; synthesizes 435 papers across six diagnostic axes and introduces the Always-On Evaluation Protocol (AOEP-v0).
*Why it matters for practitioners:* The survey's central finding — that the literature concentrates on accumulating and retrieving state but largely ignores governing, recovering, and relinquishing it — maps directly to the production gap most teams discover too late; AOEP-v0 gives you a concrete evaluation scaffold.

**Nemotron-Cascade 2: Post-Training LLMs with Cascade RL and Multi-Domain On-Policy Distillation** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19220
Shows how to extend cascade RL across math, coding, reasoning, and agentic domains simultaneously while using on-policy distillation from intermediate teacher checkpoints to recover benchmark regressions that appear when RL environments grow more complex.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* The finding that on-policy distillation from your own intermediate checkpoints (not a separate teacher model) prevents capability degradation during extended RL training is a directly applicable technique for any team doing post-training — it avoids the tradeoff between specialization and generalization.
