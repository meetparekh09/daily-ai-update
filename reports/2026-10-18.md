## Trending Papers

**MemPilot: Orchestrating On-Demand Multimodal Memory Curation for LLM Agents** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06830
Trains an RL policy to decide at each agent step whether to retrieve from pre-built memory or delegate query-specific curation of raw multimodal history to LLMs/VLMs, with credit assignment via prefix-based marginal utility estimation.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* It shows that memory retrieval should be query-aware and dynamically arbitrated rather than always pre-computed — a direct challenge to current RAG defaults in agent systems, with gains across five multimodal agent-memory benchmarks.

**Harness Engineering: Anatomy, Architecture, and Evolution of Coding Agents — A Source-Code Study of Eleven Systems** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00006
A source-code dissection of eleven production coding agent harnesses (Claude Code, Codex CLI, Gemini CLI, OpenHands, Aider, and six others), identifying seven subsystems every harness implements.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Understanding what the eleven harnesses actually share — and where they diverge — gives builders a principled vocabulary for designing the non-model parts of their agents, which the paper argues drive quality as much as the underlying model.

**When Does Muon Help Agentic Reinforcement Learning?** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16169
Finds that applying the Muon optimizer to hidden weight matrices (while using AdamW on embedding and output layers) raises final-window validation success on ALFWorld from 0.29 to 0.55 under GiGPO, an 88% improvement over AdamW.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* A single optimizer swap nearly doubles task success in sparse-reward agentic RL — a surprisingly large lever that suggests many agent RL setups are leaving significant gains on the table.

**EvoCUA-1.5: Online Reinforcement Learning for Multi-turn Computer-Use Agents** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09773
Moves computer-use agent training from offline experience learning to online RL in sandboxed environments with verifiable task outcomes, reporting 63.2% success and outperforming comparable 32B/35B open-weight models.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* It demonstrates that online RL with verifiable rewards is now feasible for long-horizon computer-use tasks, providing a concrete training recipe for teams building production CUAs.

**Distillation as Probability Transport: Routed On-Policy Distillation** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08337
Reframes on-policy distillation as a probability transport problem and introduces a routing mechanism that directs the student's distribution toward the teacher's in a principled way.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* On-policy distillation is now a dominant method for transferring reasoning from large frontier models to smaller deployable ones — a new geometric framing could change how practitioners tune the student-teacher gap.
