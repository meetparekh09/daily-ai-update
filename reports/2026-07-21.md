## Trending Papers

**SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777>
SEED converts completed on-policy trajectories into natural-language "hindsight skills" (reusable workflows, decisive observations, failure-avoidance rules) and distills their effect back into the policy as a dense token-level signal alongside sparse outcome RL.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* On-policy distillation is one of the highest-leverage ideas in agent training right now—this paper shows concretely how to turn trajectory data that would otherwise just give a binary reward into rich supervision, which is the piece most RL-for-agents implementations are missing.

**SearchOS-V1: Towards Robust Open-Domain Information-Seeking Agent Collaboration** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15257>
SearchOS applies an OS scheduling metaphor to search agents: it externalizes agent state into a Frontier Task queue, Evidence Graph, Coverage Map, and Failure Memory, then uses a Search Tool Middleware Harness to intercept model/tool interactions and recover from stalls or budget exhaustion.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Most search-agent implementations collapse under multi-hop queries because there's no explicit state management; SearchOS's decomposition of state into typed structures is an architectural pattern that generalizes to any long-horizon information-seeking workflow.

**In-Place Tokenizer Expansion for Pre-trained LLMs** — <https://arxiv.org/search/?query=In-Place+Tokenizer+Expansion+Pre-trained+LLMs&searchtype=all>
Proposes a method to add new tokens to an already-trained LLM's vocabulary without full retraining, by initializing new embeddings from existing token distributions and applying targeted fine-tuning to absorb them.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Teams adapting foundation models for code, biology, or domain-specific notation have had to either live with suboptimal tokenization or retrain from scratch; this opens a middle path that preserves existing capabilities.

**Bayesian Uncertainty Propagation for Agentic RAG Pipelines** — <https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Bayesian+Uncertainty+Propagation+Agentic+RAG+Pipelines&searchtype=all>
Treats each retrieval-and-generation step in a multi-hop RAG pipeline as a Bayesian update, propagating calibrated uncertainty forward rather than resetting confidence at each step, and evaluates on multi-hop QA benchmarks.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Agentic RAG systems that don't track retrieval uncertainty will confidently fabricate answers when early hops retrieve noisy evidence; propagating uncertainty enables downstream steps to trigger re-retrieval or abstain rather than compound errors.

**Maestro: Workload-Aware Cross-Cluster Scheduling for LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12950>
Proposes a scheduler that models agent workloads—prompt length, tool call patterns, concurrency—and routes them across heterogeneous GPU clusters to minimize latency and cost in large-scale multi-agent deployments.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* As teams move from single-agent prototypes to production fleets, naive round-robin scheduling creates hot spots and stalls; Maestro's workload-aware approach is the infrastructure piece that makes multi-agent systems reliable at scale.
