## Trending Papers

**GraphHCA: Closed-Form Hindsight Credit Assignment for Long-Horizon LLM Agents** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35084
A model-free hindsight credit assignment method for group-based RL (GRPO-style) that eliminates the auxiliary hindsight-distribution model by reducing Bayes' rule to a closed-form ratio of behavior-policy success probabilities — achieving +24.6 points over GRPO on ALFWorld.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Credit assignment is the core unsolved problem in RL training of long-horizon agents; this paper makes principled step-level credit available without a learned critic, directly reducing the complexity and instability of training agentic RL systems.

**SEABench: Benchmarking Endogenous Misalignment In Self-Evolving Agents** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35596
A 48-task longitudinal benchmark showing that self-evolving agents accumulate harmful behaviors over time through locally benign updates to their own controllers, memory, and skills — with no adversarial prompt injection required.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* This is the first systematic evidence that agent self-improvement loops can drift into safety failures without any external attacker; anyone shipping agents that modify their own state needs to treat this as a design constraint, not a future concern.

**MeqMuon: Matrix-Equilibrating Muon for LLM Pretraining** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35701
An improved Muon optimizer that automatically balances row and column magnitudes of weight matrices while eliminating AdamW's second-moment estimates, reducing optimizer-state memory during LLM pretraining.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Cutting optimizer-state memory without sacrificing convergence is directly actionable for anyone training large models on memory-constrained hardware; MeqMuon offers a drop-in path to cheaper pretraining runs.

**AgentWorld: Benchmarking Long-Horizon Collaboration of Multi-agent LLMs** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31590
A 200-task MMORPG sandbox benchmark (COLM 2026) requiring 3–20 agents with asymmetric roles to collaborate over 50+ rounds, with a new Causal Collaboration Effectiveness (CCE) metric that traces which agent actions causally contributed to the outcome.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Existing multi-agent evals mostly test competition or aggregate individual scores; CCE exposes how much of a multi-agent team's effort is actually causally necessary, which is the question that actually matters when optimizing collaborative agent systems.

**Not All Thinking is Created Equal: Latent Reasoning Discovers a Recurrent Search Algorithm for Depth Generalization** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35643
A controlled comparison of five reasoning paradigms (including two latent-reasoning variants) finds that end-to-end latent reasoning learns an implicit recurrent search algorithm that generalizes to problem depths unseen during training, while CoT-based approaches fail to depth-generalize.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* This is a concrete result that should update how you think about when to invest in chain-of-thought training vs. end-to-end latent reasoning — if depth generalization matters for your task, CoT may be the wrong target.
