## Trending Papers

**From Gradients to Capabilities: Understanding Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation** — https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent
Systematically analyzes how multiple RL-trained teacher policies influence student policy updates during on-policy distillation, identifying which gradient contributions drive capability transfer.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* On-policy distillation from RL-trained teachers is an increasingly important recipe for capability transfer; this is the first analysis of how multiple teachers interact, directly informing how to compose teacher ensembles and weight gradient contributions.

**Finetuning with Sampling: SFT Learns Better Than You Think** — https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent
Demonstrates that supervised fine-tuning with strategic sampling during training achieves strong generalization without degrading pre-trained capabilities, challenging the assumption that SFT fundamentally trades coverage for alignment.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* If this holds up, teams may be able to simplify training pipelines by replacing expensive RL stages with carefully designed SFT curricula, with significant cost and complexity savings.

**AutoCompact: Learning When to Compact Context in Long-Horizon Coding Agents** — https://arxiv.org/list/cs.AI/recent
Trains a compact policy that decides autonomously when to compress context in long-horizon coding tasks, preserving task-critical information while staying within context limits.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Context overflow is one of the top failure modes in production coding agents; a learned compaction policy outperforms fixed heuristics and avoids the manual tuning of threshold-based approaches.

**TACO: Ternary Absolute-max Column-wise One-sparse Optimizer for LLM Fine-Tuning** — https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent
Achieves up to 5× reduction in GPU memory use during fine-tuning by applying ternary sparsity to gradient state compression, with minimal accuracy degradation.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* A 5× reduction in optimizer state memory brings fine-tuning runs that previously required multi-node GPU clusters into reach on single-node setups, significantly lowering the hardware bar for instruction-tuning or domain adaptation.

**LLM2Jev: LLMs Are Already Jev-Style Decision Models** — https://arxiv.org/list/cs.AI/recent
Shows that general-purpose LLMs naturally produce categorical probability distributions over discrete options during inference, without requiring free-form text generation, making them natively suitable for bounded decision-making.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Reframes how to structure LLM-based decision nodes in agentic pipelines—the probability signal over choices is already there; many current prompting patterns discard it and then try to recover structure from text.

## Blog Posts & Articles

**"Are Agent Harnesses Dying? What Harness Distillation Changes"** — https://arize.com/blog/agent-harness-distillation/

*Why it matters for practitioners:* If scaffolding-level behaviors can be reliably distilled into weights, the long-term architecture shifts: teams currently investing in complex harness engineering are building something that will eventually be a training signal, not a runtime dependency.
