## Trending Papers

**Remember When It Matters: Proactive Memory Agent for Long-Horizon Agents** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08716
Introduces a proactive memory architecture that anticipates which past states will become decision-critical and surfaces them before they are needed, preventing context loss in long-horizon agentic tasks.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Passive retrieval — fetch context when the agent asks — still drops critical information in extended tasks; proactive anticipation is the architectural shift needed for agents that reason across hour-long workflows, and this paper provides a concrete method.

**Super Weights in LLMs and the Failure of Selective Training** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08733
Challenges the prevailing assumption that certain "super weights" are universally critical across all LLMs, demonstrating empirically that targeted training on them fails to deliver expected parameter-efficient fine-tuning benefits.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* A common PEFT shortcut — selectively targeting outlier weights — is shown not to work as advertised; this paper is a direct signal to update fine-tuning methodology rather than relying on this heuristic.

**Hierarchical Sparse Attention Done Right: Toward Infinite Context Modeling** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02980
Proposes HiLS (Hierarchical Landmark Sparse) Attention, trained end-to-end under LM loss, that matches full-attention quality in-domain while extrapolating 64× beyond training context length with >90% chunk retrieval accuracy.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* If sparse attention can genuinely match full-attention quality while enabling far longer effective contexts, it changes the cost-quality assumptions behind every long-context architecture decision currently being made for production agent systems.

**Towards Mechanistically Understanding Why Memorized Knowledge Fails to Generalize in LLM Finetuning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08393
Provides a mechanistic investigation into the internal dynamics responsible for why knowledge memorized during LLM fine-tuning consistently fails to generalize at inference time.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Anyone who has fine-tuned a model on domain knowledge and watched it fail to apply that knowledge at inference time has hit this wall — understanding the internal mechanism is the prerequisite for actually fixing it.

**The Illusion of Equivalency: Statistical Characterization of Quantization Effects in LLMs** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08734
Reveals that standard aggregate metrics (perplexity, accuracy) mask systematic behavioral shifts caused by quantization, introducing correctness-agreement metrics that expose output-level changes even when headline numbers are stable.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* If you're deploying quantized models and trusting perplexity as a quality proxy, this paper shows you should not — behavioral drift is real and invisible to the standard evaluation suite most teams use.
