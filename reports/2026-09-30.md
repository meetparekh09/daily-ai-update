## Trending Papers

**RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24972
A method from Google, Stanford, and UNC (submitted Sep 23, 2026) that adds regularization to automated harness self-improvement: a temporally annealed edit budget constrains proposals, while a critic+pruner screens and prunes selections to prevent overfitting to training benchmarks.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Unregularized harness evolution gains vanish OOD; RRSI achieves 14.1 in-distribution and 4.7 out-of-distribution point improvements across eight benchmarks (coding, agentic workspace, engineering design) with 30% fewer policy tokens—making automated harness tuning practical, not just publishable.

**NeoHorse-1: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement via Agentic Post-Training with Routing Harness** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08183
A 4B/9B model family (submitted Sep 8, 2026) that treats the deployment routing harness as a data-generation instrument: the router records each turn's capability demand, selected tier, and outcome, converting these into post-training examples via structural validation and six-dimensional semantic evaluation.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* This is the clearest concrete formulation yet of "the harness as a training flywheel"—post-training on live routing records closes most of the aggregate benchmark gap between a 4B and 9B model, with direct implications for cost-efficient production fine-tuning loops.

**Counterfactual Rollout Replay: Forkable Environments as Free Process Rewards for Software Engineering Agents** — https://arxiv.org/search/?searchtype=all&query=counterfactual+rollout+replay+forkable+environments
A NeurIPS 2026 accepted paper showing that snapshotting a coding environment once and forking every RL rollout from that snapshot drops environment setup time from ~20 hours to ~4.4 hours across 8,000 rollouts.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* The technique delivers 2.01× and 1.55× end-to-end wall-clock speedups on SWE-bench Verified for Qwen3-14B and Qwen3-32B—RL training for code agents just became feasible on meaningfully smaller compute budgets.

**CHILL-Harness: Counterfactual Harness Learning for Efficient Reasoning in Long-Horizon Agents** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25825
A method that constructs matched trajectory pairs from a shared interaction prefix to identify which harness components are causally responsible for reasoning quality in long-horizon tasks, then prunes the non-causal ones.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Knowing which context elements actually drive outcomes (vs. noise) gives teams a principled way to reduce per-call token cost in production agents without degrading task performance—something prompt compression heuristics cannot do reliably.

**Training Object Permanence in World Models** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28654
A 30-author paper from CMU, Berkeley, Stanford, NYU, and others (Sep 23, 2026) introducing WROP—150 Blender-generated task families and a 1.5M-sample training corpus designed to teach video world models to track occluded objects; their 16B PWM-WROP model ranks first among continuation-style video models in blind Elo evaluation.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* WROP provides the first standardized benchmark for measuring whether a world model maintains object state across occlusion—a prerequisite capability for reliable embodied and robotic agent planning.
