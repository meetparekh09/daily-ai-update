## Trending Papers

**NCP-ArchPreview: Moving towards Latent Space Language Models through Next Concept Prediction** — [arxiv.org/abs/2609.10715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10715)
An 8.94B-parameter foundation model that augments next-token prediction with next concept prediction over discrete multi-token spans, reaching OLMo-3-7B's final pretraining loss while consuming only 51.3% of the training tokens and outperforming it by 2.45 points on downstream benchmarks.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* This is the clearest demonstration yet that "what the model predicts at training time" is a first-class architectural lever—not just tokenization or scale—and a senior ML engineer should update their pretraining intuition after reading it.

**SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning** — [arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777)
A framework where the current policy checkpoint serves simultaneously as the rollout actor and the trajectory analyzer: it extracts natural-language hindsight skills from completed trajectories, then distills the skill-induced probability shift back into itself as a dense token-level on-policy signal alongside outcome-based RL.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* On-policy distillation is the dominant pattern in 2026 frontier post-training; SEED shows how to make it self-evolving for agent tasks without a separate teacher model, improving both performance and sample efficiency.

**Trains but Doesn't Learn: A Post-Training Delivery Benchmark for LLM Agents as Forward-Deployed Engineers** — [arxiv.org/abs/2609.25237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25237v1)
A benchmark that exposes a sharp gap between LLM agents optimized on standard training metrics and their ability to generalize as forward-deployed software engineers solving novel, real-world tasks—models "trained" to high scores consistently fail at the deployment distribution.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* If you're shipping agents into production with training-time evals as your confidence signal, this paper is a direct warning that the evals are measuring the wrong thing.

**Agent Memory Distillation: Empowering Small LLM Agents with Hierarchical Teacher Memory** — [arxiv.org/abs/2608.07169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07169)
A training-free framework that transfers structured memory—including reusable workflows, observations, and failure-avoidance rules—from a large teacher agent to a small student agent through hierarchical memory compression.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Enables cost-efficient agent deployments where a small model inherits the accumulated experience of a frontier model, without fine-tuning or distillation infrastructure.

**What is Missing from AI Post-Training: An Empirical Analysis** — [arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19072](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19072)
An empirical audit of contemporary post-training pipelines that identifies systematic gaps—specific capability domains and failure modes that current RLHF/DPO/distillation recipes consistently fail to address.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Provides a concrete checklist of post-training blind spots that every team running RL-based fine-tuning should benchmark against before declaring a model "ready."
