## Trending Papers

**ShiftRAG: Bypassing the Textual Bottleneck via Decoupled Learning and Continuous Soft Tokens** — [arxiv.org/abs/2611.00871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00871)
A NeurIPS 2026 paper that separates discriminative retrieval training from autoregressive generation on a shared LLM backbone, then passes retrieval signals to the generator as continuous soft tokens rather than text.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* The soft-token channel delivers retrieval information at higher fidelity than any text representation can — this directly attacks the accuracy ceiling in standard RAG pipelines, with reported gains on both retrieval and downstream QA benchmarks while preserving the backbone's general capabilities.

**Termination Lag: Coding Agents Solve Early but Stop Late** — [arxiv.org/abs/2611.14960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14960)
An empirical study across five models, two agent frameworks, and three SWE-bench variants showing coding agents continue working for a median 35–60% of their total steps *after* they have already produced a correct patch.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* These "post-solution steps" waste compute and in 1–8% of cases lead agents to undo their own correct fix — knowing this, practitioners should instrument their pipelines to detect correct-patch checkpoints and add explicit early-stop signals rather than letting agents run to natural completion.

**MEMENTO: Learning Context Management for Long-Context Agents** — [arxiv.org/list/cs.CL/current](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.CL/current)
A cs.CL paper proposing a learned policy for deciding what an agent retains, summarizes, or discards from its context window across long interaction horizons.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Brittle heuristics — sliding windows, manual summarization triggers — are the current norm for long-context agents; a trainable context-curation policy could replace ad-hoc rules with an approach that adapts to task structure.

**Sparse Teacher-Student Scoring for Cheaper Forward-KL Distillation** — [arxiv.org/list/cs.CL/current](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.CL/current)
A cs.CL paper reducing the cost of forward-KL on-policy distillation by sparsifying the dense scoring computations between teacher and student models at each training step.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* On-policy distillation is expensive enough that most teams only run it once; if sparse scoring holds accuracy, this lowers the bar for iterative distillation cycles — directly relevant to anyone compressing large reasoning models into faster serving checkpoints.

**LoRA Fine-Tuning Leaks More Training Domain Than Previously Assumed** — [arxiv.org/list/cs.CL/current](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.CL/current)
An early-November cs.CL paper measuring the degree to which LoRA fine-tuning embeds detectable training-domain fingerprints into model weights, finding leakage is larger and more persistent than commonly modeled.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Teams using LoRA on sensitive corpora (medical notes, proprietary code) need empirical evidence for how much of the source distribution bleeds through — this paper provides a calibration baseline for assessing that risk.
