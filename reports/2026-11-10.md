## Trending Papers

**"Frontier Models Can Interpret and Modify Their Own Weights" (GPT-6 Astra weight interpretation)** — <https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/3/qwen-can-interpret-model-weights/>
A paper showing that frontier models, specifically GPT-6 Astra, can both read and modify the raw weights of an ML model, with Willison replicating the finding locally on Qwen 3.8 27B.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* If sufficiently capable models can self-modify their weights, it breaks the assumption that training and inference are strictly separate phases—with large implications for fine-tuning pipelines and AI safety.

**"Sparse Autoencoder Attribution Graphs for Mechanistic Interpretability" (arXiv:2611.00606)** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00606>
Uses sparse autoencoders and causal attribution to construct interpretable circuit graphs showing how model features compose and interact.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Circuit-level visibility into how features compose gives teams a practical tool for debugging unexpected agent behavior and verifying that reasoning steps are grounded.

**"Improved Speculative Decoding via [method]" (arXiv:2611.00588)** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00588>
A new speculative decoding method that improves draft acceptance rates, reducing per-token latency for LLM serving.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Faster inference at the same quality directly reduces cost and improves responsiveness for latency-sensitive agent loops.

**"Soft-Token Retrieval: Continuous Representations for Dense Passage Retrieval" (arXiv:2611.00871)** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00871>
Proposes replacing discrete chunk retrieval with continuous soft tokens, allowing the retriever and generator to share a richer representational space.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* This challenges the standard RAG assumption that retrieval and generation operate over the same discrete text; adopting soft-token retrieval could improve answer quality for complex multi-hop queries.

**"Sparse Attention Head Selection as a Jailbreak Defense" (arXiv:2611.08752)** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08752>
A defense mechanism that identifies and selectively activates sparse attention heads during inference to block jailbreak attempts.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Architecturally-grounded defenses that work at the attention-head level are more robust than prompt-based guardrails, and could be integrated into production serving with low overhead.
