## Trending GitHub Repositories

*Why it matters for practitioners:* At v3.11.1 with 175 merged PRs and 52 community contributors, this is a working blueprint for multi-platform agentic research with built-in trust signals — the self-verification layer (re-fetching Polymarket lines, GitHub stars, StockTwits sentiment at act time) is the pattern production agent teams need to adopt to avoid stale-fact hallucination.

## Trending Papers

**A Few Teacher Steps Go a Long Way: Cost-Efficient On-Policy Data Augmentation for Agent Post-Training** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04574>
A July 6 Stanford/NYU paper that reframes on-policy SFT for LLM agents as a budget-allocation problem, showing that a few teacher-generated continuations placed at learner-induced (not teacher-induced) states beat full teacher demonstrations or heavily filtered completions at matched cost.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* If you're fine-tuning an agent with teacher rollouts, this paper tells you exactly where to spend the budget — short continuations at student-induced contexts outperform end-to-end demos across HotpotQA, ALFWorld, and Terminal-Bench-Dev, making on-policy agent training significantly cheaper without sacrificing quality.

**AgentArk: Distilling Multi-Agent Intelligence into a Single LLM Agent** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03955>
A framework that distills the reasoning and self-correction dynamics of multi-agent debate into a single model's weights through three hierarchical strategies: reasoning-enhanced fine-tuning, trajectory-based augmentation, and process-aware distillation.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Multi-agent systems are accurate but inference-expensive; AgentArk demonstrates that you can shift the compute cost from test time to training time, yielding a single agent that achieves multi-agent quality on HotpotQA, QASPER, TruthfulQA, and multimodal benchmarks — the distilled models also transfer to unseen tasks.

**When Does Muon Help Agentic Reinforcement Learning?** — <https://kimbodo.com/ai-research-papers-july-20-2026/>
An empirical study finding that replacing AdamW with the Muon optimizer (applied only to hidden weight matrices) in sparse-reward agentic RL raises final validation success from 0.290 to 0.546 (+88%) on ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct under GiGPO, with gains that depend on the advantage estimator and learning rate.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* The optimizer is treated as a fixed default in virtually all agentic RL work; these results demonstrate that Muon's spectral orthogonalization is particularly well-suited to policy gradient training in long-horizon sparse-reward tasks — a concrete hyperparameter update for anyone running agentic post-training.

**MirrorCode: AI can rebuild entire programs from behavior alone** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30182>
An Epoch AI benchmark that evaluates AI agents' ability to reimplement entire software projects (Unix utilities, bioinformatics toolkits, interpreters, cryptography tools) given only the original program's behavior — no source code — with AI scored on whether output matches held-out end-to-end tests.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Frontier models already score 56% on complex multi-thousand-line projects (including a 16,000-line bioinformatics toolkit), meaning AI-driven legacy migration and behavior-based reverse engineering are significantly closer than most teams assume — the benchmark's per-task cost data ($2,600/attempt for the largest tasks) makes capability estimates actionable for planning.

**HAS-Bench: Evaluating LLM-Based Human-Agent Systems under Configurable Human Participation** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04329>
A benchmark that evaluates how LLM-based agent systems perform across a configurable range of human participation modes — from fully autonomous to heavily supervised — on a suite of realistic tasks.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Most agent benchmarks assume a fixed automation level; HAS-Bench reveals how agent performance profiles shift with different human engagement modes, which is the actual decision every production team must make when deciding where to keep humans in the loop.
