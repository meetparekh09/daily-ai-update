## Trending Papers

**"Can Compact Language Models Search Like Agents? Distillation-Guided Policy Optimization for Preserving Agentic RAG Capabilities"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20324
A two-phase approach (cold-start distillation followed by RL with selective KL penalties) that trains compact 0.5B–1B models to perform agentic RAG on par with much larger teachers; accepted to ACL 2026.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Shows that small models can inherit agentic search-and-reasoning behavior through structured distillation before RL, which makes agentic RAG viable in cost-constrained production deployments.

**"SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777
An on-policy distillation framework where the student continuously updates its own teacher signal during RL training rather than relying on a fixed larger model.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Addresses the instability of agentic RL by keeping the distillation target aligned with the student's evolving policy, a direct improvement over trajectory-level reward-only training that any agent RL practitioner would want to benchmark against.

**"MemCalib: Benchmarking and Optimizing Memory Use in LLM Agents"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24259
A benchmark that measures how much influence each memory placed in an LLM agent's context actually receives, plus an optimization method to calibrate that influence.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Reveals that LLMs do not weight memories proportionally to their relevance, which undermines many naive RAG designs — the benchmark and optimizer let teams diagnose and fix this without replacing their memory architecture.

**"Efficient and Transferable Agentic Knowledge Graph RAG via Reinforcement Learning"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26383
An RL-trained approach for coupling LLMs with structured knowledge graphs in a RAG pipeline, designed to reduce the inference cost and brittleness of fixed multi-module KG pipelines.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Demonstrates that RL-trained retrieval policies generalize across knowledge graphs at lower cost than fixed module stacks, which matters for any team building domain-specific agents over structured data.

**"The Compliance Trap: Diagnosing How AI Agents Consume Conflicting Memory"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10608
A diagnostic study (Johns Hopkins / Cornell) of how agents behave when conflicting facts are injected into memory, finding systematic failure modes that do not appear in standard benchmarks.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Exposes a class of memory-integrity bugs that existing evals miss, with concrete test cases that agent developers can adopt immediately to check their own systems.
