## Trending Papers

**Reasoning Shift: How Context Silently Shortens LLM Reasoning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01161
An empirical study (COLM 2026 Spotlight) showing that reasoning models produce up to 65% shorter traces when given lengthy irrelevant context, multi-turn conversation history, or subtask framing — with decreased self-verification and uncertainty management.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* If you're deploying reasoning models in agentic pipelines where context accumulates across turns, your model is silently degrading its own reasoning depth in ways that won't show up in standard benchmarks — this tells you why and how much.

**Beyond Imitation: Reflective On-Policy Self-Distillation for LLM Reasoning** — https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent
Proposes a self-distillation approach that combines on-policy generation with explicit reflection mechanisms, going beyond simple behavioral cloning from teacher traces.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* On-policy distillation (a method the reader tracks closely) has a core limitation: it still imitates outputs rather than learning to reflect on them; this paper attacks that gap directly and is worth comparing against standard STaR/SPIN baselines.

**Understanding On-Policy Distillation through the Lens of Test-Time Scaling** — https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent
Provides a theoretical and empirical bridge between on-policy self-distillation and test-time compute scaling, with public code.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* The two dominant paradigms for improving reasoning — train-time distillation and test-time scaling — are increasingly shown to be two sides of the same compute trade-off; this paper makes that relationship precise and gives practitioners a framework for choosing where to invest.

**EpiKV: Epiphany-Aware KV Cache Eviction Without the Attention Matrix** — https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent
A KV cache eviction strategy that identifies and retains "epiphany tokens" — tokens associated with key reasoning transitions — without computing the full attention matrix.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Efficient long-context reasoning is a hard deployment problem; approaches that don't require re-computing attention to decide what to evict are more practical at inference time, and this framing (preserve reasoning pivots, drop the rest) is a principled heuristic rather than an approximation.

**AgileLog: A Forkable Shared Log for Agents on Data Streams** — https://arxiv.org/list/cs.MA/current
Accepted at SOSP 2026, this systems paper introduces a forkable shared-log abstraction for coordinating multiple agents processing streaming data concurrently.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Most agent coordination primitives assume batch or request/response patterns; SOSP acceptance signals this is a production-grade systems contribution for teams building agents over live data pipelines — the forkability primitive in particular enables branching agent execution without log duplication.
