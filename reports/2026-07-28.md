## Trending Papers

**Distilled Reinforcement Learning for LLM Post-training** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17247
Proposes a new post-training objective called Distilled RL that integrates teacher logit supervision directly into the RL objective, replacing the binary choice between outcome-supervised RL (coarse credit assignment) and on-policy distillation (blind KL matching that fails when teacher and student diverge significantly).
*Why it matters for practitioners:* This is the most direct answer yet to the on-policy distillation vs. RL debate — it changes the post-training design space by showing that fine-grained teacher guidance and RL exploration are complementary, not competing; any team currently running post-training should read this before their next experiment.

**GRASP: GRanularity-Aware Search Policy for Agentic RAG** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10463
Trains an RL agent to adaptively coordinate multiple retrieval tools (semantic search, keyword search, paragraph-level reading) during multi-step reasoning, with a reward function that penalizes redundant search and rewards grounded, complementary retrieval actions.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Most production RAG systems still hardcode their retrieval strategy; GRASP demonstrates that learned retrieval policies outperform fixed pipelines on multi-hop QA, and the framework is close enough to production to adapt directly.

**InferenceBench: A Benchmark for Open-Ended LLM Inference Optimization by AI Agents** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20468
A benchmark that gives an AI agent one H100, a target LLM, and a two-hour wall-clock budget to deploy and optimize an OpenAI-compatible inference server across four bottleneck scenarios (prefill latency, decode latency, throughput, and mixed).
*Why it matters for practitioners:* The surprising finding — agents reliably beat naive PyTorch baselines (up to 8×) but are outperformed by simple hyperparameter sweeps over vLLM/SGLang defaults — reveals both the capability floor and ceiling of autonomous infra optimization agents today.

**Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench: Testing the Limits of Agents on Long-Horizon Terminal Tasks** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08964
A benchmark of hard, realistic command-line tasks requiring dozens of sequential steps, with dense reward-based grading to provide training signal on long-horizon agent trajectories.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Existing agent benchmarks are saturating; this benchmark surfaces a clear capability gap (long-horizon CLI tasks), providing both a measuring stick and training data shape for teams building coding and DevOps agents.

**Neuron-Aware Data Selection for Annotation-Free LLM Self-Distillation** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02460
Proposes a data selection method that identifies which training examples activate the most distinctive neurons in a student model during self-distillation, eliminating the need for human annotations while improving distillation data efficiency.
*Why it matters for practitioners:* Annotation-free self-distillation is directly relevant to teams that want to compress models cheaply; this gives a principled criterion for sample selection that beats random filtering without any labeling cost.
